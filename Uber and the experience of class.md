There is a logic that has inscribed itself so much into my brain that it generates certain affects that appear central to how I understand differences in class. The logic goes: Ubers are taxis, taxis are expensive, so people in taxis must be rich. Therefore, if I use Uber, I must be rich. It's a crude logic, but it kinda feels like that nonetheless.

Recently, I went to see a dance performance, and I went there (and back!) with Uber. I just tapped a few buttons on my phone, the car pulled up in front of my house, I got in the back and marveled at the city lights through the tainted glass, before I got out again at the theater. On the way back, I repeated the whole thing. 

Maybe it was the combination of those rides with the fact that I went to a classic culture-capital-heavy activity. But somehow, spending the night as described, I felt *so adult*, so independent, almost noble, lofty, wealthy. Maybe separate from the rest. Perhaps even a little higher? Better...?

Obviously, I am quite far away from believing that those who can afford hyper-individualist transportation (driving is one thing, driving alone is already worse, but *being* driven‽‽) are better in any kind of way. But it still goes to show how in my socialization, there is a whole imaginary to this kind of transportation, and to those who can attain it. Stepping out of my own national context and into that of Brazil, I retain this imaginary, but can suddenly participate in it–for that night, I briefly, if only ever so slightly, changed my social class, experienced upwards mobility. Uncomfortably, hand in hand with that perception goes an idea of being somehow better. As if I deserved to be in that car, more so than someone else.

I draw a bunch of reflections from this. First of all, class is not just an economic position but also a performance. I can borrow a certain posture, appropriate symbols and practices, and with this newly acquired *habitus*, embody a position I don't normally hold. Elite-coded transport is just one out of many, but surely a strong signal of class.
On the other hand, it's also not *just* a performance–in this example specifically, the material conditions set the scene. In other words, me sitting in those Ubers (me being in Brazil, me having time (and a taste!) for theater, etc) already requires significant financial capital in the first place.

Second, class is also relational. The tainted glass window separated me from the city passing by, and much of what I saw appeared, relative to my position, poorer. This impression itself surely has much to do with the way capital mediates public visibility. Still I wonder, finding myself in a position somehow higher than another–who is this other? One of the people behind the car window, maybe the guy who slept on the sidewalk we drove past? Or closer still, only separated by a car seat–the driver in front of me, who was working while I was sitting back and enjoying the view? He is probably working right now, as I write these lines.

In any case, I was definitely not comparing myself to those who spent their night in much more expensive ways, who may even have personal drivers (or helicopters, for that matter). This points to the unfortunate political tendency to kiss up and kick down, the strange gravitational pull that makes us gaze downward toward those with less, rather than upward toward those with more. Conveniently for the latter, this means that any discomfort class differences provoke rarely translate into pressure on those who profit most, while instead, solidarity becomes harder to foster.

Last but not least, class is a changer of perception. The fact that I have a critical stance towards classism does not mean I completely stand outside of the affects it invokes. The feeling of elevation remained somewhat intact, though observed disgustedly by internal commentary, it was still somewhat immune to the critique.
This raises some questions about the meaningfulness of what a lot of us are keeping ourselves busy with (aka becoming some sort of well-educated pseudo critical theorist that has carefully constructed ample progressive opinions on sociopolitical issues), and how effective that is in changing the conditions, be they material or "just" emotional. In other words, why did I manage to write this elaborate text but did not manage to distance myself entirely from those feelings? Even worse, does this whole text just serve to show off my understanding of class, in an insidious attempt to accumulate even more cultural capital? (Only exacerbated by this infinitely loopable self-reflexivity?)

Since I am not sure about the actual value of this essay, I will cut this stream of thought short here, and leave it to you to decide whether this text only contributes further to the classist project, or if it stirs up something for you. Perhaps you will catch a similar feeling sometime, in the same or in a completely different context, of being somehow above–or just as well, below!–someone else. If you do, let me know what you think, and maybe some conversation will come of it that actually helps someone.
