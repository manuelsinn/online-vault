People keep saying this to me so I googled:

"Manuel" is actually the Spanish/Portuguese adaptation of the Greek adapation Μανουήλ of the Hebrew name עִמָּנוּאֵל‎ (Immanuel), which means "God is with us". The Hebrew original was adapted to Greek in the Byzantine Empire because they spoke Greek, were Christian and liked (Hebrew) biblical names. 

So basically, my name's origin story is essentially composed of the more or less violent spread of Christian gospel and culture throughout the world, and arrived in Brazil after an especially violent process of colonization. However, since it's been used in this country for around 500 years it's become thoroughly nativized, which I guess... counts?
