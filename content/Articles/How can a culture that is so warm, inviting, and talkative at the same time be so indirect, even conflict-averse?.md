## It's complicated

Often when I talk with Brazilians about things that are outright shit - that make me angry, sad, annoyed or invoke any other unpleasant reaction - I yearn for someone to call it by its name. I thirst for people to express their discontent. What I hear instead in many cases is the phrase "tá complicado" (it's complicated). And it leaves me RAGING! Aren't some things simply unjust, depressing, and annoying? And I am not even talking about disagreements and multiperspectivity, of course there are different angles to issues. But even if someone agrees with me, they would say, well yeah... it's complicated.
To be fair, more recently the expression is growing on me: I gained a feeling for its weight, which arguably even increases by the additional weight of not naming the cause for disliking something. As if both speakers shared the knowledge of how severe something is, and perhaps even a similar conviction on what the cause for it is, yet need not (cannot?) speak it out loud.

## Do you accept sugar?

For example, I have been asked the question "aceita açucar?" (do you accept sugar?). What sounds a little strange at least and may be misunderstood as an inquiry about one's argumentative stance on the stimulating substance is, of course, just a simple offer that follows a serving of coffee.

I find this curious because in this scenario, to ask if the someone might *accept* something that is offered seems like a further inversion of responsibility: As if the sole act of *affirming that you would like something* (not even voicing it by yourself) is already a burden that one should avoid placing on someone else's shoulders. Instead, the interaction is framed as if the waiter were to bring the coffee, the sugar, the water to the guest *anyway*, and the guest is simply tasked with *accepting* it, reducing any possible inconvenience to the minimum. 

Another nice example is the use of the word "interessante". When I asked strangers about the security of a neighborhood and whether they deem it necessary to order an Uber to get to the bus station, they said "seria interessante pegar um Uber" (it would be interesting to take an Uber). Of course the guy didn't talk about Uber rides being intellectually stimulating but rather framed his advice (which I even asked for directly!) in a super indirect way. Consider this spectrum:
you must take an uber - you should take an uber - it would be good to take an uber - it would be interesting to take an uber - ??

(To be fair, I guess it's kind of like "it wouldn't be a bad idea to take an Uber at this time of night" which is arguably even more indirect)
