## What surprised me?

- the amount of VW Bus and VW Käfer
	- It's rare to spot the iconic T2 Bulli in Germany and Europe, not least due to its highly priced collector's value. Here in São Paulo, I see one almost every day (check out this ridiculous police edition).
	- While German production stopped in 1979, Brazil continued to produce 20k models per year for over 50 years until 2013 - no wonder they still roam every street! The fact that they feel so retro and have been present in the country literally when it was *still a military dictatorship* constructs a curious continuity between the times.

- the size of avocados

- soft toilet seats - when you sit down, the seat gives off a gentle "FFFfff..." as you *sink into it*
  
- how normal it has become for me NOT to throw toilet paper in the toilet (btw, not flushing paper is [globally more prevalent](https://web.archive.org/web/20170917021932/http:/wheredoiputthepaper.com/) and thus arguably the "normal" way of doing things)
  
- Paulistanos are even better at [queueing]() than the Brits (video vorm bandejao! 4.3.)

- how often I hear that my name is Brazilian (and how people start to sing a [song]() that has the name - and that there's basically a song to almost every name)
	- "Manuel" is actually the Spanish/Portuguese adaptation of the Greek adapation Μανουήλ of the Hebrew name עִמָּנוּאֵל‎ (Immanuel), which means "God is with us". The Hebrew original was adapted to Greek in the Byzantine Empire because they were Christian and liked (Hebrew) biblical names. So basically, my name's origin story is essentially composed of the more or less violent spread of Christian gospel and culture throughout the world, and arrived in Brazil after an especially very violent process of colonization. However, since it's been used in this country for around 500 years it's become thoroughly nativized, which I guess... counts?
